**Problem and method.** The current rule requires a configured concession verb before it can start, counts negation anywhere after a requirement anchor within a coarse clause, and treats an unscoped responsibility noun as an extension. Keep the existing legal-domain/requirement/polarity configuration and rule identifier. Represent each relevant statement as a source span with clause boundaries, grammatical subject/object attachment, predicate and auxiliary-negation scope, and authored/reported/quoted status. **Requirement state.** Tie a requirement anchor to its governing predicate rather than every nearby negative word. Connective/adnominal/adverbial grammar, intervening subject/object markers and quotation boundaries delimit the predicate attachment. Negation of an intervening action or of a later limited conclusion must not negate a fulfilled requirement. Requirement predicates may contain auxiliary negation or nominalized existence denial; an unbound mention, competing subject, nested scope or incomplete parse remains uncertain. Preserve configured absent-fact polarities. Do not add words that suppress warnings or extend negation lists to match supplied/private expressions.

**Conclusion and speaker.** Check each authored conclusion's scope separately using configured categorical and limited conclusion spans plus clause structure. A second authored effect outside the limited debt conclusion cannot borrow that conclusion's limitation. Quotations and opponent reports are evidence of what another speaker said, not author fulfilment/denial; a later rebuttal or independently asserted conclusion must be evaluated on its own span. Preserve existing normal defenses and polarity behavior for other rules. An uncertain requirement supporting an asserted limited conclusion retains the existing rule as a requirement-verification request; uncertainty must not silently erase a warning.
